How the models read the market

The LVX models do not forecast prices. They read the shape of recent price action: every security is reduced to a detrended, smoothed curve, compared point by point with the same curve of the index, and ranked on what is left. A second, slower curve of the index itself decides how much of an account is in stocks at all. The parameters of both were found by evolutionary search over decades of data.

01   Signal construction

  1. Detrend. Each price is expressed against its own 200-session mean, so that securities at any price level and in any regime share one scale. The open is measured against the mean known before that open.
  2. Smooth. The detrended series is passed through a penalised smoother (second-difference penalty, Whittaker type). The right end is pinned to the latest observations so the curve cannot drift from where the price actually is.
  3. Sample. Slope and height are read at 50 equally spaced points along the smooth curve.
  4. Compare. The index goes through the identical pipeline. A security is scored on its slope profile relative to the index and on the dispersion of the combined curve.
  5. Rank. Scores are converted to cross-sectional ranks among the largest companies by market value; the account holds the best-ranked names and reviews them on a fixed cycle.

03   Exposure control

A separate rule sets the share of each account held in stocks. The index is detrended the same way and smoothed over a ten-year window; the slope at the end of that curve is the regime signal. Exposure changes only when the signal has turned and the index has moved by more than a set band since the last change, with confirmation and post-crash conditions that filter out whipsaws. The rule is evaluated at the close and applied at the next open.

04   Evolutionary search

No gradient training is involved. Each candidate is a complete configuration of the two rules above. A generation consists of the surviving parents and their mutations; every candidate is simulated end to end as a trading account, scored, and the best become the next parents.

05   Point-in-time discipline

  1. Every signal uses data up to that day's close only. Smoothing is one-sided: the curve ends at the decision point and is never revised with later data.
  2. Order sizes are computed from the decision day's close; fills are taken at the next session's open with slippage.
  3. The research set contains delisted securities, so the simulations are not built on survivors.
  4. The live implementation was checked against the research implementation on the same day: rank correlation 0.9999 across common securities.
  5. Live records are append-only. A processed session is never rewritten.

06   Known limits

Historical simulation is not evidence of future results. Much of the simulated edge in recent years comes from a small number of exposure changes, and the daily excess return over the index is not statistically distinguishable from zero over short windows. The six models are close relatives and overlap heavily. The only clean test is the live record that began on 2026-11-17, which is why it is published as it happens.

Parameters, window lengths and holdings are not disclosed. Simulated accounts, no real money. Nothing here is investment advice.
